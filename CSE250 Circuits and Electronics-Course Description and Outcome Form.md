2. **Course Catalog Description (Content):** 

This course provides a comprehensive introduction to linear electric circuit analysis, fundamental electrical concepts, and fundamental measuring units, combining theoretical mathematical modeling with mandatory weekly laboratory experimentation. The curriculum begins with the definition of basic electrical parameters (charge, voltage, current, energy, and power), the passive sign convention, and the physical I-V characteristics of ideal circuit components, including independent and dependent sources, resistors, capacitors, and inductors. Students examine fundamental governing laws, such as Ohm's law, Kirchhoff's current law (KCL), and Kirchhoff's voltage law (KVL), alongside circuit reduction methods including series and parallel equivalence and voltage and current divider rules. The course then covers systematic network analysis techniques, developing proficiency in nodal and mesh analysis for multi-loop direct current (DC) circuits containing both independent and dependent sources. Core linear circuit theorems, including superposition, source transformation, Thevenin and Norton equivalent circuits, and maximum power transfer, are analyzed to simplify complex electrical networks. Finally, the curriculum explores time-domain transient analysis of first-order RC and RL circuits, followed by alternating current (AC) steady-state circuit analysis using sinusoidal sources, phasor representations, and complex impedance concepts. A compulsory 3-hour weekly laboratory session reinforces theoretical concepts through hands-on circuit construction, measurement, and troubleshooting using standard equipment such as multimeters, oscilloscopes, and function generators.

3. **Course Objective**   
1. Introduce fundamental electrical quantities (voltage, current, power), energy definitions, and the passive sign convention for electrical circuit analysis.  
2. Illustrate the terminal I–V characteristics of two–terminal devices and ideal circuit components, including dependent and independent voltage and current sources, resistors, capacitors, and inductors—to infer circuit equivalence.  
3. Explain governing circuit laws, including Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL), Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL), and Ohm's Law, alongside basic reduction techniques such as series–parallel equivalence and voltage–current divider rules.  
4. Apply systematic circuit analysis techniques, specifically nodal analysis and mesh analysis, to solve multi-loop linear direct current (DC) circuits containing independent and dependent sources.  
5. Demonstrate linear network theorems, including superposition, source transformation, Thevenin–Norton equivalent circuits, and the maximum power transfer theorem to simplify and analyze complex circuits.  
6. Analyze the time-domain transient response of first–order RC and RL circuits, as well as the steady–state AC behavior of linear networks using phasor representation and complex impedance concepts.  
7. Train students to construct, test, and troubleshoot linear electrical circuits in a weekly laboratory setting using standard electronic components.  
8. Develop students' proficiency in using standard laboratory instruments—such as digital multimeters, oscilloscopes, and function generators—to measure and verify physical circuit parameters.  
9. Enhance teamwork and technical communication skills through collaborative laboratory experimentation and structured technical report writing.
